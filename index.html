toppletee

making golf difficult in all the wrong ways

ToppleTee is a golf game where the golfer's entire body becomes the controller. Instead of just choosing a direction and stopping a power meter of some kind, players adjust individual joints, select a club, aim, and hope their posture is good enough to send the ball where they intended. Just like when I play golf IRL.

The idea

I came up with ToppleTee after repeatedly failing to hit a decent drive while my wife tried to correct my posture. Most golf games remove that physical frustration, so I wondered what would happen if a game made it the central mechanic. Every small adjustment to the golfer's knees, shoulders, elbows, and hands affects the resulting shot.

What I did

I designed and developed ToppleTee as a solo project for Reddit's Games with a Hook hackathon. I built the posture and swing systems, procedural course generation, ball physics, club behavior, daily and practice modes, interface, art, and Reddit Devvit integration.

Design process

The central challenge was translating joint positions into ball physics in a learnable way, while still balancing frustration with fun. Each club uses its own ideal posture profile, while the game evaluates the player's pose and translates its mistakes into changes in distance, accuracy, and shot shape. Feedback after each swing helps the player understand which part of their posture had the greatest effect.

Reflection

ToppleTee taught me how much iteration it takes to make intentional frustration feel playful. The most successful moments happen when a shot goes terribly wrong for a reason the player can recognize, followed by the brief triumph of finally getting everything to line up.

SketchTiler

Co-creative sketch-to-tilemap generation

A level ideation tool designed to lower the barrier to entry for tile-based level design. Users sketch a rough layout, and the tool translates that sketch into a full tilemap using Wave Function Collapse.

What I did

I handled the translation layer from sketch data to tile data, and redesigned the WFC algorithm using a hierarchical approach, bringing generation time down from around 10 seconds to under 1.

Design goal

The goal was to make the ideation phase faster and more intuitive, especially for designers who aren't already fluent in tile-based level design.

AstroFrame

A roguelike built around ship building, combat, and exploration.

AstroFrame is a 2D roguelike built in Godot around ship building, combat, and exploration. As design lead, I architected spreadsheet-driven data pipelines to manage game state and balance, keeping the full system legible and iterable without touching engine code.

Narrative and systems

As narrative lead, I wrote the world's history from the ground up, including faction lore, characters, and the stories that give the universe texture. I also worked on the roguelike side, designing randomization systems that keep runs feeling fresh.

Mind Palace: Running Out of Time

A procedural mystery with no fixed truth.

A game jam project tackling one of the harder problems in procedural generation: the mystery. Inspired by the failures of real-world criminal justice, where innocence and guilt aren't always what determine a verdict, the game casts the player as a lawyer who constructs a case from whatever evidence they find.

Generated evidence

Characters are generated with interests, jobs, and relationships, then assigned roles that shape what evidence exists to be discovered. A job might place someone at a scene; a relationship might become a motive.

Design outcome

Despite the difficulty in crafting a procedural mystery, I count the player's agency as one of our design wins; the truth is yours to make.
